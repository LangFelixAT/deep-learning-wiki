## Scope
This ingest covers grouped-query attention as a middle point between [[Multi-Head Attention]] and [[Multi-Query Attention]], sharing key/value heads across groups of query heads, the quality/speed motivation, the relationship between query heads and key/value heads, high-level uptraining from multi-head checkpoints, KV-cache and memory-bandwidth effects, and why GQA matters for efficient LLM inference.

Excluded: detailed benchmark tables, full T5 training setup, implementation details, hyperparameters, tokenizer/data details, and modern MLA/FlashAttention/paged-attention details except as future connections.

## One-sentence summary
Ainslie et al. introduce grouped-query attention, which shares each key/value head across a group of query heads to get much of the decoding efficiency of multi-query attention while preserving quality closer to multi-head attention.

## Why this paper matters
The paper gives a source-grounded bridge between standard multi-head attention and multi-query attention, and it frames key/value head count as a tunable design axis for LLM decoding efficiency.

## Problem
Autoregressive decoder inference is slowed by memory bandwidth from repeatedly loading attention keys and values during decoding. [[Multi-Query Attention]] reduces this cost by using one key/value head, but the source says MQA can degrade quality and may be undesirable to train separately from a high-quality MHA model.

## Core idea
Grouped-query attention divides query heads into `G` groups. Each group shares one key head and one value head.

This interpolates between:
- `GQA-1`: one key/value group, equivalent to [[Multi-Query Attention]];
- `GQA-H`: one key/value head per query head, equivalent to [[Multi-Head Attention]] when there are `H` query heads.

The paper writes `GQA-G` for grouped-query attention with `G` key/value groups.

## Method
- Start with a Transformer checkpoint using multi-head attention.
- Convert it to MQA or GQA by reducing key/value heads.
- For GQA, group query heads and mean-pool the original key/value projection heads within each group.
- Continue pretraining for a small fraction of the original pretraining budget so the model adapts to the new attention structure.
- In the paper's encoder-decoder setting, GQA is not applied to encoder self-attention because encoder representations are computed in parallel and key/value memory bandwidth is not the same bottleneck.

## Important equations
Let:
- `H` be the number of query heads;
- `G` be the number of key/value groups.

Then:
- MHA has $G = H$ key/value groups.
- MQA has $G = 1$ key/value group.
- GQA uses `1 < G < H`.

Each key/value group serves approximately `H / G` query heads.

Relative to MHA, the number of stored key/value heads is reduced from `H` to `G`, so the KV-cache head dimension is reduced by a factor of roughly `H / G`.

Needs verification: exact tensor layouts and speedups depend on implementation, sharding, sequence length, and hardware.

## Results
- Claim from source: uptrained GQA achieves quality close to MHA while having speed close to MQA in the reported experiments.
- Claim from source: uptraining from existing MHA checkpoints can produce MQA/GQA models using a small fraction of original pretraining compute.
- Claim from source: mean-pooling key/value heads works better for checkpoint conversion than selecting a single head or random initialization in the paper's ablations.

Detailed benchmark tables are outside this ingest scope.

## Limitations
- Claim from source: the paper evaluates GQA and uptraining only on encoder-decoder models.
- Claim from source: the paper does not compare the XXL GQA model to a comparable model trained from scratch due to limited computation.
- Claim from source: evaluating long-generation quality is difficult, and Rouge does not tell the whole story for summarization.
- Modern decoder-only LLM behavior should be checked against later sources.

## Claim from source
- Claim from source: MQA drastically speeds up decoder inference but can lead to quality degradation.
- Claim from source: GQA generalizes MQA by using an intermediate number of key/value heads.
- Claim from source: GQA-1 is equivalent to MQA, and GQA-H is equivalent to MHA.
- Claim from source: GQA reduces KV-cache size and loaded data compared with MHA.
- Claim from source: GQA gives a favorable tradeoff for larger models because it avoids the aggressive capacity reduction of MQA while retaining much of the bandwidth benefit.

## My interpretation
GQA turns key/value sharing into a knob. Instead of choosing between full MHA quality and full MQA cache savings, the model can choose how many key/value groups to keep.

This makes attention efficiency feel like a design-space axis: query heads govern how many attention patterns the model can ask for, while key/value heads govern how much memory state must be stored and read during decoding.
