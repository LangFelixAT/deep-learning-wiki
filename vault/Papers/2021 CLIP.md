# Learning Transferable Visual Models From Natural Language Supervision

## Metadata
- Authors: Radford et al.
- Year: 2021
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00020
- Source type: paper
- Status: studied
- Reliability: high

## Scope
This ingest covers only core conceptual ideas: joint image-text embeddings, contrastive learning over matched and mismatched pairs, cosine/dot-product alignment, semantic embeddings, and why such embeddings can serve as conditioning signals.

## One-sentence summary
CLIP learns a shared embedding space for images and text by training image and text encoders to align matching pairs and separate non-matching pairs.

## Problem
Standard visual models are often trained on fixed label sets, limiting transfer to new visual concepts; natural language supervision offers a more flexible way to describe visual concepts.

## Core ideas
- Learn an image encoder and a text encoder jointly.
- Map images and text into a shared embedding space.
- Treat each true image-text pair in a batch as a positive pair.
- Treat other image-text combinations in the batch as negative pairs.
- Use cosine similarity / dot product between normalized embeddings as the alignment score.
- Natural language can refer to visual concepts through the learned text embedding space.

## Important equations
Image and text embeddings:

$$
i = \operatorname{normalize}(f_{image}(image))
$$

$$
t = \operatorname{normalize}(f_{text}(text))
$$

Pairwise similarity logits:

$$
\operatorname{logits} = \frac{i t^T}{\tau}
$$

Contrastive objective, conceptually:

$$
\max \operatorname{sim}(image_i, text_i) \quad \text{and} \quad \min \operatorname{sim}(image_i, text_j) \text{ for } j \ne i
$$
The paper uses a symmetric cross-entropy loss over image-to-text and text-to-image matching scores.

## Claims from source
- Claim from source: predicting which caption goes with which image is an efficient way to learn image representations from natural language supervision.
- Claim from source: CLIP trains image and text encoders by maximizing similarity of real pairs and minimizing similarity of incorrect pairings in a batch.
- Claim from source: after pretraining, natural language can reference learned visual concepts for zero-shot transfer.
- Claim from source: CLIP uses cosine similarity between learned image and text embeddings as the matching score.

## Limitations
- This note does not include architecture details, implementation tricks, dataset-scale details, or training infrastructure.
- The paper's empirical transfer results are not ingested here beyond the source claims above.
- Needs verification: downstream use of CLIP-like embeddings for generative conditioning is an interpretation connecting CLIP to later generative systems, not the main focus of this paper note.

## My interpretation
CLIP-style embeddings are useful for generative conditioning because they turn language into semantic vectors that can be supplied to another model as conditioning information.
